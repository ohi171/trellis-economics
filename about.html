About Trellis

Independent research grounded in method, context, and use.

Trellis Economics brings together applied economic research and global development data practice to help organizations produce evidence that is credible, transparent, and decision-ready.

Our purpose

Build the structure that good evidence needs.

A trellis supports growth without determining its shape. Our role is similar: create the research design, data systems, quality checks, and analytical structure that allow credible evidence to emerge.

We work with public institutions, international organizations, NGOs, universities, think tanks, and funders on questions where methodological rigor and practical relevance must coexist.

Team

Complementary experience across economics and data.

Our founders combine causal and policy-oriented economic research with global data systems, indicators, quality assurance, and evidence communication.

Working principles

How we protect the credibility of the work.

Good analysis depends on more than a model. Our principles shape project design, collaboration, and delivery.

Fit the method to the question

We distinguish descriptive, predictive, and causal goals before selecting analytical tools.

Make assumptions visible

We document key decisions, limitations, sensitivity checks, and areas of uncertainty.

Protect data and participants

We use ethical data-handling practices appropriate to sensitive health, household, and administrative information.

Build reproducibly

We organize code, data transformations, documentation, and outputs so the work can be reviewed and extended.

Communicate for the audience

We preserve technical accuracy while adapting outputs for researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and funders.

Collaborate where expertise is needed

We partner with qualitative researchers, field teams, and subject specialists when a project requires broader capacity.

Work with us

Looking for an analytical partner rather than a black box?

We collaborate closely with client teams, explain methodological choices, and leave behind work that can be understood and used.
