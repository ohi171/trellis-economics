Expertise

Economic and social questions across connected systems.

Our work spans health, human development, climate, labor, migration, education, poverty, gender, and economic policy—with a particular focus on South Asia and low- and middle-income settings.

Sectors

Methods and data

The method follows the question.

We distinguish descriptive, predictive, and causal objectives and choose analytical tools accordingly.

Causal and econometric methods

  • Difference-in-differences
  • Event-study designs
  • Instrumental variables
  • Fixed-effects and panel models
  • Sample-selection correction
  • Decomposition methods
  • Rare-events logistic regression

Predictive and spatial methods

  • LASSO and regularization
  • Random forest
  • XGBoost
  • Cross-validation
  • Performance assessment
  • Geospatial data linkage
  • Spatial exposure measurement

Data and research practice

  • Household-survey analysis
  • Administrative and international data
  • Longitudinal and repeated cross-sections
  • Data harmonization and validation
  • Indicator construction
  • Robustness and sensitivity analysis
  • Reproducible workflows

Tools

Analysis that can be reviewed, rerun, and extended.

Our workflows use established statistical and data tools selected for the project context and the client's ability to maintain the work.

  • Statistical analysisStata, R, and Python.
  • Geospatial analysisQGIS and spatial workflows in R and Python.
  • Data communicationPower BI, Excel, publication-ready graphics, and presentation materials.
  • Technical productionLaTeX, structured documentation, versioned code, and reproducible outputs.

Analytical principle

Credibility before complexity.

We use the simplest design that can answer the question responsibly, document every important assumption, and separate what the evidence establishes from what remains uncertain.
